A client called last month. Her son's admission deposit was due in three weeks: ₹6 lakh. Her bonus would arrive in nine months. She had ₹20 lakh in equity mutual funds and her first instinct was to redeem.

We ran both options side by side.

## Option A: Redeem ₹6 lakh

- **Tax.** Say ₹2 lakh of the ₹6 lakh is long-term gain. After the ₹1.25 lakh annual exemption, ₹75,000 is taxed at 12.5%. About ₹9,400.
- **Lost growth.** If the market returns 12% a year, ₹6 lakh out of the market for nine months misses roughly ₹54,000.
- **Behaviour.** Money that leaves a portfolio rarely comes back in full. Bonuses find other uses.

## Option B: Borrow ₹6 lakh against the funds

- **Interest.** At around 10.5% a year for nine months: about ₹47,000.
- **No tax event.** Units stay invested. SIPs continue.
- **Limit.** Lenders typically allow up to about 50% of equity fund value. Her ₹20 lakh supports a ₹6 lakh loan comfortably.

On these numbers, borrowing cost ₹47,000. Redeeming cost about ₹63,000, plus the risk of never rebuilding. She borrowed.

## The four questions that decide it

**1. How long do you need the money?** Under 12 to 18 months, a loan usually wins. Beyond that, interest compounds against you and redemption often makes more sense.

**2. Is the repayment certain?** A confirmed bonus or a maturing FD, yes. "Business should pick up," no. If repayment is uncertain, do not borrow.

**3. What is the tax on redemption?** Large unrealised gains tilt toward a loan. Funds near cost, or in a loss, tilt toward redeeming.

**4. Can you handle a margin call?** If markets fall 25%, the lender may ask for more security or partial repayment. Keep the loan well under the limit so that never happens.

## Where people go wrong

They treat the loan as permanent. A loan against mutual funds is a bridge, not a lifestyle. We set a repayment date on day one and put it in the client's calendar.
