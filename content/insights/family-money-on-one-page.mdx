Your family's money fits on one page when you list every investment, policy and loan in one place, then add four things most lists leave out: **what each one is for, when you will need it, who the nominee is, and who at home knows where this page is.**

Most families can tell you roughly how much they have. Far fewer can tell you where all of it is. That gap matters most on the day someone else has to find it.

**In short**

- One page beats a long spreadsheet, because the person who doesn't manage the money can still read it.
- Start with three columns you already know (investments, insurance, loans), then add four you probably haven't written down.
- A Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) finds your mutual funds. It won't find your insurance, loans or provident fund.
- A blank on the page isn't a failure. It's where to start.
- Review it once a year, and after any marriage, birth, move, new loan or death in the family.

## Why one page, and not a spreadsheet?

A spreadsheet is built by the person who manages the money, for the person who manages the money. It grows tabs, formulas and abbreviations that only make sense to them.

One page forces a different question: could your spouse, or your grown-up child, pick this up on a bad day and know what to do next? If the answer is no, the money is organised for you, not for your family.

## What goes on the page?

Seven lines. The first three you probably know. The last four are the ones families tell us they have never written down.

| Line | What to write | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Investments | Mutual funds, shares, FDs, PPF, EPF, NPS, gold, property | The full picture, not just the accounts you look at often |
| 2. Insurance | Life, term, health, and any old policies still running | Old policies are the ones most often forgotten |
| 3. Loans | Home loan, car loan, loans against policies or funds | What's owed matters as much as what's owned |
| 4. What it's for | Retirement, a child's education, emergencies, nothing specific | Money without a job tends to get spent or ignored |
| 5. When you'll need it | A year, or "anytime" | Shows what has to stay safe and what can stay invested |
| 6. Nominee | The name on each investment and policy | Nominees are often outdated, missing, or different across accounts |
| 7. Who knows | Who at home knows this page exists, and where it is | The most important line, and the one most often blank |

## How do you find everything you hold?

You probably don't need to remember everything. Most of it can be found from statements you can request yourself.

- **Mutual funds:** a Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) lists the funds held under your PAN across fund houses. You can request one through the registrars (CAMS and KFintech) or MF Central. The depository version, from NSDL or CDSL, also shows shares and bonds in your demat account.
- **Insurance:** the policy documents, the insurer's annual premium statements, or the insurer's website or app. If your policies are held in an electronic insurance account, that lists them in one place.
- **Loans:** your bank statements show the EMIs. A credit report from a credit bureau also lists the loans and cards in your name.
- **Provident fund and NPS:** the EPFO passbook and your NPS statement.
- **Older or forgotten investments:** for mutual fund folios with no activity for years, SEBI introduced a tracing tool called MITRA in 2025, run by the registrars. {/* VERIFY: current MITRA access and steps on the official portal before publishing. */}

## The mistakes we see most

- **Nominees that never got updated.** Added years ago, before a marriage or a death in the family, and never checked since.
- **Money with no job.** Funds bought for a goal that has since changed, or for no stated goal, sitting untouched.
- **Old policies nobody at home knows about.** Premiums paid by standing instruction for years, documents in a cupboard.
- **Old contact details on folios.** A mobile number or email that no longer works, so statements and alerts go nowhere.

## Who at home should know, and how?

At least one other adult should know the page exists and where it's kept. Talk them through it once. It takes about twenty minutes.

Keep the page somewhere they can reach without your phone or your passwords. Don't write passwords or full account numbers on it. The provider names, the last four digits of each account and the nominee are enough for someone to start.

## How often should you update it?

Once a year is enough for most families, and the weeks before 31 March are a natural time, because statements are being gathered for tax anyway. Update it sooner after a marriage, a birth, a move, a new loan, a new policy, or a death in the family.

## Questions people ask

**Isn't a CAS enough?**
A CAS is the best place to start for mutual funds and demat holdings. It doesn't show insurance, loans, provident fund or bank deposits, and it doesn't say what each investment is for or who at home knows about it.

**Should I write account numbers on the page?**
The last four digits are enough. Don't write passwords or PINs on it.

**What if I find a blank I can't fill?**
That's the point of doing it. A blank shows you where to start, whether that's a missing nominee, a policy nobody can explain, or a fund with no job.

**Do I need a distributor to do this?**
No. Everything above can be done yourself. Some families prefer to sit with someone who does this every week, and that's what our first meeting is.
